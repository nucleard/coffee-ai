Farm climate reports

A pipeline that pulls four decades of daily satellite and reanalysis weather for any set of coordinates, corrects it for the farm's real elevation, and writes an analysis a farm can use, covering rainfall and dry spells, warming trends, day-night temperature range, harvest and drying windows, flowering triggers, and disease risk.

This page runs the first half of it live. Pick a coffee region or type a farm's coordinates and elevation, and it pulls every day since 1981 from the ERA5 reanalysis. The second half, interpreting the data for the farm, is the part I do myself.

How a report is made

Most farms have no weather station and no history. The data exists, but it's raw, gridded, and at the wrong elevation. The numbers then have to be interpreted by someone who knows the farm.

Sources
NASA POWER and Open-Meteo, with 40 years of daily ERA5 data as the main source.
Elevation
Each grid cell has its own elevation, which is usually different from the farm's. Temperatures are corrected for the difference at the standard rate of 6.5 °C per 1,000 m.
Analysis
Python does the statistics. AI drafts the narrative from the finished numbers, and I edit it.
Output
Two documents per farm: a short executive summary and a full report with a section per question.

Data on this page: ERA5 reanalysis via the Open-Meteo historical weather API, fetched by your browser when you press the button. Nothing is stored. Temperatures are corrected from the terrain elevation Open-Meteo reports to the farm elevation you enter, at 6.5 °C per 1,000 m. Trends are ordinary least squares on annual values; the R² is printed next to each trend so you can see how strong it is.
