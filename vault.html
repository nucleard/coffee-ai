How it works

    
I have cupped professionally for twenty years and most of those notes are gone. They were on paper sheets, in an app that closed, and in spreadsheets that didn't match each other. Every cupper has this problem. The conventional fix is either a slow form or someone's proprietary software. I wanted something I'd actually use at the table, and that would still be readable in twenty years.

    

      
Capture
Photograph the sample bag or tray and talk. Formal SCA scoring and a casual impression both work.

      
Names
Coffee names get mangled by speech-to-text. A short protocol at the start of each dictation, plus a lookup against the vault's lot list, keeps "Suke Quto" filed as "Suke Quto".

      
Filing
Claude turns the transcript into a structured note with descriptors, scores if spoken, defects and context. It records only the numbers that were said.

      
Linking
Notes link to the green lot, the roast batch, and previous cuppings of the same coffee, so a lot's whole history is one click away.

      
Format
The archive is a folder of markdown text files that any tool can read. There is no database and no vendor.

    

    
Every cupping I've done since developing this in October 2025 is searchable. I'm even able to add in old notes I find in a drawer or on an ancient Google Drive. I can ask what a coffee tasted like six roasts ago and get my own words back. The roastery query system grew directly out of this.
