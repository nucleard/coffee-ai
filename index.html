Since teaching myself how to code in 2020, I have been diving deeper and deeper into the ways that computing can help the coffee industry. Working with frontier AI models, with agentic architecture and coding, and running open-source models locally, I have developed a suite of tools, from the eminently practical to the highly speculative, with the same goal: harness the power of artificial intelligence in sane, approachable ways that actually benefit people who work in coffee and which lead to a better business and better coffee.

This page demos some of the concepts I've developed and which are actually in use by coffee professionals today. Each project rests on a deeper code base which is available upon request. Again, the emphasis is on tools which actually get used, which are easy to access, and which actually help coffee professionals do their jobs.

01

Gas use dashboard

Built with the University of Amsterdam, 2026

Some people associate AI with wasting energy. Properly applied, AI can optimize systems and find ways to use significantly less energy than we are already currently using (not some theoretical energy-use in the future). That was the idea behind this project: to roast the same profile with less gas.

In 2026 Coffee & Coconuts took part in AI in je Bedrijf, a programme that pairs small Amsterdam companies with student teams from the University of Amsterdam and the Amsterdam University of Applied Sciences. A team of four business IT students built a dashboard for the gas use of our Giesen 15.

  • It reads the roast exports from Cropster, splits each roast into drying, Maillard and development, and totals the gas in each phase.
  • Each roast's temperature and gas curves are compared with a reference roast of the same coffee, and every batch is ranked on gas use and on how closely it followed the profile.
  • Roasts are linked to the weather on the day and to moisture and density readings of the green coffee.
  • A model reads the first minutes of a roast and predicts its total gas use and how far it will drift from the profile.

02

The roastery query system

In use by the roastery team since August 2026

A question-answering system over everything a roastery knows about its coffee: roast data, webshop orders, green quality readings, cupping notes, customer records and meeting notes. Anyone on the team asks from their phone and gets an answer that quotes the source.

  • A small model turns the question into a fetch plan and plain Python runs the plan. A second model writes the answer from the fetched records and names the file each part came from.
  • Python does the arithmetic from the order ledgers, and the answer says what was excluded.
  • When the records don't hold the answer, the system says "I don't know".

03

The cupping vault

Every cupping filed since October 2025

Photograph the sample, talk through the cup, and the note lands in a searchable, cross-linked archive. I have cupped professionally for twenty years and most of those notes are gone. They were on paper sheets, in an app that closed, and in spreadsheets that didn't match each other.

  • Coffee names get mangled by speech-to-text, so each dictation is checked against the lot list.
  • The model turns the transcript into a structured note and records only the numbers that were said.
  • The archive is a folder of markdown text files that any program can open, so it does not depend on a database or on one company's software.

04

Farm climate reports

Gesha Village, Ethiopia · Fazenda Pedra Preta, Brazil

Forty years of satellite and reanalysis weather for any coffee farm, corrected for the farm's real elevation and turned into a report a farm manager can act on: rainfall and dry spells, warming trends, day-night temperature range, harvest and drying windows, flowering triggers and disease risk.

  • Python does the statistics. AI drafts the narrative from the finished numbers, and I edit it.
  • The demo page pulls the daily record for any coordinates and draws it while you wait.

The coffee evaluation corpus

Research database, growing nightly · counts from 26 October 2026

A record of what people say about the flavour and quality of many, many different coffees. It can be cross-indexed to find whether varieties, origins, altitudes, roast levels and processes are described consistently, in Cup of Excellence juries and in ordinary shops.

  • A language model running on one graphics card in my house reads each product page overnight and extracts the coffee, its descriptors and its price.
  • Every extracted word is checked against the page it came from. I chose the model by testing it against a hand-reviewed answer key. All of its descriptors matched the page word for word, against 42% for the faster model.
  • It respects robots.txt and AI-crawler opt-outs.

06

The vault galaxy

A 3D map of a roastery's notes

Every note in the roastery's records is drawn as a point of light, and every link between two notes as a thread. Notes about similar things sit close together, so the coffees, the customers, the roasts and the cuppings each form a region.

  • Each note's position comes from the words in it. A word-embedding model places the notes, and UMAP reduces the result to three dimensions.
  • Tags are drawn as points too, linked to every note that uses them.
  • It runs in a browser from one HTML file and one data file.

How I build these

In all of these systems, Python does the calculations and a language model writes up the results. Each answer names the record it came from, and the records are kept as plain text files. I capture notes by voice and by photo, at the cupping table and on the roastery floor. I built all of it myself with AI coding tools while working as a roaster.
